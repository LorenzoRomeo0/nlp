Representation bias · Bhagat et al. (2026), TALES

Knowing a culture is not the same as portraying it.

  • The test
    540 stories of about 1,000 words, set in Indian regions, in English and 13 Indian languages, from six models. 108 annotators from 71 regions flagged 2,925 problematic passages, sorted with a taxonomy built with Indian participants.
  • The finding
    Errors go beyond stereotypes: cultural mistakes, clichés, oversimplifications, implausible scenes. They are more frequent in languages with fewer data and in stories set in small towns and rural areas.
  • Knowing versus applying
    Models often answer correctly about traditions they misrepresented in their own stories (TALES-QA: 1,683 questions drawn from the errors). The knowledge seems to be there, but is not mobilised when narrating.
  • A trade-off
    Some models make fewer errors partly because they include fewer specific cultural references.
